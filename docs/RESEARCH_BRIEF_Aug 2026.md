## 1. Spacesuit Technology Landscape

### AxEMU Status (schedule, budget, testing)
- Axiom won the xEVAS contract (June 2022); Artemis III lunar-surface task order up to **$228.5M**. Combined xEVAS contracts (Axiom + Collins) up to **$3.1B** — NASA rents suits, doesn't own.
- Feb 2026: Axiom closed a **$350M** round (Type One Ventures, QIA); **>$850M raised to date**.
- Design partners: **Prada** (materials/aesthetics), **Oakley** (visor, 2025). ~8.2 psi operating pressure, 8+ hr EVA, rated ≥2 hr at −230 °C, 1st–99th percentile fit, dorsal entry, HUD.
- Schedule: NASA delayed crewed lunar landing to **2028**; AxEMU is that suit. Axiom targets late-2027 demo readiness; OIG warns slip to **2031**.
- Testing: **850+ hrs pressurized testing with person inside** (Feb 2026); critical design review; first flight parts assembling spring 2026; NBL + ARGOS lunar-gravity + thermal-vac testing; training suits delivered.
- Sources: NASA OIG IG-26-006 (Apr 2026), NASA milestone article (Feb 12 2026), Orbital Radar, Axiom Space.

### SpaceX EVA Suit (capabilities, limitations)
- First flown Polaris Dawn, **Sep 12 2024** (first commercial spacewalk, ~700 km). Evolved from Crew Dragon IVA suit; 3D-printed helmet w/ HUD + HD cam; scalable design; Mars/Starship focus.
- SpaceX IPO June 2026 (~$1.75–2.5T valuation).
- Limitations: **umbilical-based life support** (not self-contained PLSS) → unsuitable for lunar/planetary surface; Crew Dragon has no airlock (whole cabin depressurizes); not sold externally.
- Sources: Orbital Radar, Polaris Program, SpaceNews.

### New Competitors
- **Axiom** (frontrunner, $850M+), **SpaceX** (Mars focus, not bidding lunar), **Collins/RTX+ILC Dover+Oceaneering** (xEVAS task orders descoped 2024; NASA spent $37M), **ILC Dover** (OIG-named on-ramp candidate), **Boeing** (Starliner IVA), **Final Frontier Design** (low-cost entrant), **Genesis** (OIG on-ramp candidate), **Orlan-MKS** (Russia), **Feitian** (China).
- Sources: NASA OIG, Market Research Future, CNBC, studyguides.com.

### Key Market Gaps (where BioArmor fits)
1. **Single-source / no-backup risk** — Axiom only qualified provider; OIG slip-to-2031 leaves 50-yr-old EMUs flying. Opening for a 2nd certifiable supplier.
2. **Radiation shielding gap** — June 2026 *Acta Astronautica* PVA/BA hydrogel self-heals in 48h, cuts SPE dose >60% at ~1cm; Mar 2026 *Advanced Materials* 3D-printable stretchy shield. Active field seeking breakthrough BioArmor could own.
3. **Puncture / MMOD self-healing** — suits "collapse if punctured." Self-healing polymers researched but not flight-integrated. Armored self-sealing outer layer = direct BioArmor value prop.
4. **Cost & scalability** — modern suits up to ~$12M each, bespoke. Need orders-of-magnitude cheaper, mass-producible.
5. **Inclusive sizing & rapid fit** — EMU fixed HUT sizes cancelled 2019 all-woman EVA. Modular parametric armor shell = unmet.
6. **Lunar dust & interoperability** — abrasive regolith unsolved; OIG flags missing interoperability standards.
7. **On-orbit maintainability & longevity** — repairable modular subsystems differentiator.

## 2. Algae Bioreactor & Life Support

### ISS Photobioreactor Status
- **PBR@LSR** (DLR/IRS/ Airbus) launched Q2 2019, *Chlorella vulgaris*, first hybrid (PC+biotech) demo. **Ended after ~2 weeks** from power-supply failure; samples returned SpX-18. Intended pairing: PBR@ACLS.
- **Arthrospira-B** (Spirulina) flew 2018; feeds ESA MELiSSA.
- **Space Algae-1/2**: Space Algae-2 plans 5–6 month continuous *Arthrospira platensis* on ISS; cryopreservation method published Apr 2026 (*npj Microgravity* 12:56).
- Momentum: *Algal Research* special issue (Jun 2026); IAC-2025 closed-loop paper.
- Status: only short-duration sub-scale algal flights; no 180-day+ success. Hybrid (PC+biology) is consensus path.

### Ground-Based Bio-Regen
- **Lunar Palace 365** (Beihang): 370-day, **100% O₂/water recycling, 98.2% closure** (*Acta Astronautica* 228:131, 2025).
- **BIOS-3** (Russia): 10 crewed closure tests, longest 3-person/180 days (1972–73).
- **MELiSSA** (ESA): 5-compartment loop; pilot plant in Barcelona; 2026 multi-month integrated test planned. **No ESA-owned crewed BLSS facility.**
- **EDEN ISS** (DLR, Antarctica): year-round plant food analog.
- **NuCLEUS**: not confirmed in 2025–26 sources — treat as unverified; canonical European programs are MELiSSA/EDEN ISS.

### Wearable Algae Systems
- **TAPED** (*ACS Nano*, Apr 25 2025): self-powered algae wearable (TENG harvests biomechanical energy) for O₂ supplementation in hypoxia — circumvents direct implant risks. Body-worn O₂ *production* exists only in early medical-research form, not flight/EVA hardware.
- Most 2026 "wearable O₂" products are sensors, not generators.

### Sizing Requirements
- *Chlorella*: up to 4 g/L/day; PBR for 10–30% food needs ≈ 50–100 L/person. Break-even vs PC resupply at 4–7+ yr missions.
- *Chlorella* yields ~1.6 g O₂/L/day; full personal O₂ demand (~0.84 kg/day) needs **~500 L dense culture** — far beyond suit capacity. Algae = supplement, not sole source.

### Key Technical Challenges
Reliability/power (PBR@LSR 2-wk failure); microgravity fluid dynamics; radiation effects on viability; contamination/biofilm; O₂ inhibition/photoinhibition; cryopreservation/restart (Space Algae-2 solved *A. platensis* via 10% DMSO + 10% trehalose at −80 °C, 1-yr viability); automation/gas balance; mass/power penalties.

### Implications for BioArmor design
- Concept validated, not flight-ready. Adopt **hybrid architecture** (compact PBR + PC loop).
- Suit can host only a **partial-supplement** PBR (tens of L max) — size as augmentation/emergency reserve.
- **TENG self-powering** (from TAPED) → autonomous EVA module, avoids battery drain.
- Species: *Chlorella vulgaris* or *Arthrospira platensis*; include **cryopreserved starter cache**.
- Reliability must lead: redundant power, contamination isolation, graceful fallback to PC O₂.
