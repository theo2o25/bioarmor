### 1) Company & Team (~85 words)

BioArmor is a pre-seed hard-tech startup building a second-source, low-cost, self-healing EVA armor system. We address the single-supplier risk flagged in NASA OIG IG-26-006 (Apr 2026): Axiom AxEMU is the sole qualified lunar/ISS suit through 2031, with 50-year-old EMUs as the only backup. Founders [NAME — materials/CNT-aerogel + bio-regenerative PBR + EVA systems — TO COMPLETE] and advisors [JSC EVA / DLR PBR@LSR / MELiSSA — via Sep 8 Moon Base RFI]. We will be <500 employees, US-owned, PI >51% effort, SAM.gov UEI active.

### 2) The Problem & Customer Discovery (~220 words)

NASA rents suits under xEVAS (up to $3.1B combined, Axiom $228.5M Artemis III task order) but OIG warns AxEMU readiness may slip from late-2027 to 2031, and Collins xEVAS was descoped ($37M spent). Legacy EMUs fly beyond design life. OIG explicitly invites a second certifiable supplier (ILC Dover, Genesis named).

Seven gaps remain unmet: (i) MMOD puncture depressurization — suits collapse if punctured; (ii) SPE radiation — needs >60% dose cut at ~1 cm (Acta Astronautica Jun 2026); (iii) $12M bespoke cost, no mass production; (iv) fixed HUT sizing (canceled 2019 all-woman EVA); (v) lunar dust abrasion; (vi) missing EVA interoperability standards; (vii) on-orbit repair.

Market: NASA lunar/ISS EVA + commercial LEO (Axiom Station, Starlab) + DoD dual-use protective systems. A 10× cost cut unlocks certification plus DoD licensing.

Why now: Four materials just hit readiness — DexMat Galvorn CNT fabric ISO 9001 (Aug 2026, 3000× scale, cost –99.6%, >8×10⁵ S/m, –80% wire weight, NASA TechPort 93594), Oxford PV 24.5% perovskite shipping, NASA-validated Surlyn 8940 self-healing bladder (LAR-TOPS-122/224, Patent 11,192,667, seals ~2 mm, 10–50 cycles), and knittable HANF aramid aerogel fiber (Nat. Commun. Mar 2026, 22 mW/m·K, 30–133 MPa, 5.4 °C warmer than cotton, washable). None have been flown together. Timing aligns with DARPA R6 (Oct 21) and DOE fall SBIR.

### 3) The Innovation & Technical Approach (~310 words)

BioArmor integrates those four into a modular 3-layer armor + hybrid bio reserve, TRL 3→4 in Phase I. EAP joint assist (DEA MCP cuff 19.5 kPa, 0.7 s) is TRL 3–4 and scoped as Phase II stretch only.

Layer 1 — Outer shell: DLP alumina tiles (Lithoz LithaLox 350, 99.8% Al₂O₃, 400 MPa, 97.5% density) or Galvorn CNT shell, parametrically sized (1st–99th percentile without new HUT). Ceramic AM removes ~80% machining cost. CNT provides EMI shielding.

Layer 2 — Self-healing bladder: Surlyn 8940 + Barex + thiol-ene, two-tier healing (melt + O₂ polymerization). Validated to seal ~2 mm holes near-100% for small punctures. Gamma degradation mitigated by Barex barrier and coupon screening. Not yet flight-integrated — that is the innovation gap we close. Pass criterion: 2 mm nail puncture at 8.2 psi (AxEMU pressure), 50 cycles, leak <1%/hr.

Layer 3 — Thermal liner: HANF aerogel fiber, machine-knit, dust-tolerant vs. legacy silica aerogel, solves brittleness/washability.

Hybrid O₂ reserve: 5–10 L Chlorella/Arthrospira PBR, TENG self-powered (TAPED, ACS Nano Apr 25 2025, harvests biomechanics, avoids battery drain), with cryopreserved starter (10% DMSO + 10% trehalose at –80 °C, Space Algae-2, npj Microgravity 12:56, 1-yr viability). Sized as supplement/emergency only — full human demand needs ~500 L at 1.6 g O₂/L/day (Frontiers 2021). Architecture: compact PBR + physico-chemical loop, redundant power, contamination isolation, graceful fallback. Addresses PBR@LSR 2-week power failure mode.

Phase I (6 mo, $305K) builds five 30×30 cm coupons and tests per NASA profile (850+ hr pressurized history, NBL/ARGOS/thermal-vac analogs): puncture, thermal-vac, radiation screening, and 5 L PBR bench (O₂ yield + power budget) plus manufacturability cost model proving 10× path. Budget: materials $80K, Lithoz print $40K, PI+engineer 0.5 FTE $120K, testing $40K, overhead $25K.
